How much does it cost to sell a home in Bogotá in 2026?

Commission, notary fees, withholding tax and capital gains tax: what comes off the price before the money reaches your account.

Johana Bolaños · November 2026

When people sell their apartment, they usually think about the sale price. What really matters, though, is the net amount: what's left after commissions, fees and taxes. These are the items worth calculating before you set your price.

1. Brokerage commission

No Colombian law sets the percentage; what counts is what's agreed in writing in the brokerage contract. If nothing was agreed, local commercial custom applies: the Bogotá Chamber of Commerce recognizes a commission of 3% of the sale price for urban properties in Bogotá, paid by the seller, plus VAT.

2. Half of the notary fees

By custom, buyer and seller each pay half of the notary fees. For a standard (non-social-housing) sale, the seller's half is roughly 0.27% of the price, plus VAT and copies.

3. Withholding tax

If you sell as an individual, 1% of the price is withheld when the deed is signed. It isn't a sunk cost: it's an advance on your income tax, credited later in your tax return.

4. Capital gains tax

If you owned the property for two years or more, the gain (sale price minus tax basis) is taxed as an occasional gain at 15%. If it was your primary residence, the first 5,000 UVT of gain may be exempt (about COP 261,870,000 with the 2026 UVT), as long as the money goes into an AFC account to buy another home or to pay off the mortgage on the property sold.

An example with numbers

Sale of a COP 400 million apartment by an individual, with a 3% brokerage agreement:

Commission (3%, before VAT)COP 12,000,000

Capital gains taxDepends on your gain and exemption

Other common costs: cancelling the mortgage if there's still a loan, being up to date on property tax and HOA fees, and getting the certificates the notary requires.
