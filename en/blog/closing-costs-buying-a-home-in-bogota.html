Closing costs when buying a resale home in Bogotá

Besides the price, buyers pay notary fees, registration tax and fees, and, with a mortgage, some bank costs. Here's how they add up.

Johana Bolaños · November 2026

One of the most common mistakes when buying a home is budgeting only for the price and the down payment. At closing, additional costs appear that together usually add up to 2% to 3% of the property's value.

What the buyer usually pays

  • Half of the notary fees: about 0.27% of the value, plus VAT and copies. The seller pays the other half.
  • Registration tax: in Bogotá, 1% of the sale value.
  • Registration fees at the Public Records Office, according to the rate table of the Superintendency of Notaries and Registry.
  • Title search: a legal review of the property's history, usually between COP 350,000 and 700,000.

If you buy with a mortgage

Add the bank's appraisal, its own title review, the mortgage deed and registration, and the insurance tied to the loan. Ask the bank for a written breakdown before approval.

An example

For a COP 300 million apartment, a cash buyer's costs can be around COP 6.5 to 7.5 million. With a mortgage, the total goes up.

How to avoid surprises

  1. Ask for a recent certificate of title (certificado de tradición y libertad) before signing the purchase agreement.
  2. State in writing in the agreement who pays each cost.
  3. Set aside at least 3% on top of the price in your budget.

When I advise a buyer, I provide the true total cost of the purchase before any offer: price, closing costs, financing and estimated monthly payment.

General information updated November 2026. Rates and rules change; confirm your specific case with the notary, your bank or a tax advisor.
