Documents you need to sell your apartment in Bogotá

Having your paperwork ready from the start builds buyer confidence and keeps the sale from falling apart at the notary. Here's the basic list.

Johana Bolaños · November 2026

A serious buyer, and their bank, will review the property's legal status before signing. Having everything ready from day one speeds up the sale and builds trust.

Basic checklist

  • Public deed through which you acquired the property.
  • Recent certificate of title (certificado de tradición y libertad). Notaries and banks usually want one issued within the last 30 days.
  • Property tax clearance and, if applicable, betterment levy clearance.
  • HOA fee clearance issued by the building administration.
  • ID of every owner.
  • Condominium bylaws or the reference to their deed.

Special cases

  • If there's a mortgage: ask the bank for the payoff amount and the letter to cancel it in the same sale deed.
  • If it's registered as family housing (afectación a vivienda familiar): that status must be lifted, and by custom the seller pays those costs.
  • If you inherited it or it's in probate: the probate process must be finished first.
  • If you live abroad: you can sell through a special power of attorney granted at a Colombian consulate.

A tip

Review the certificate of title before listing. If it shows a lien, an old mortgage that was never cancelled, or an error in the area, it's better to fix it before a buyer shows up.

General information updated November 2026. Rates and rules change; confirm your specific case with the notary, your bank or a tax advisor.
